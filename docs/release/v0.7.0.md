# v0.7.0 Stone Age — the Avatar factory (Beta)

The author selected **v0.7.0 Stone Age**. VUA stays in Beta. This release carries the first real
Avatar production path: VUA takes an Avatar and outfits from your library, builds them into a
working Avatar in its own Unity project, and uploads it to VRChat after you confirm.

Other editions: [简体中文](v0.7.0_ZH.md) · [日本語](v0.7.0_JA.md) · [한국어](v0.7.0_KO.md)

## Download and install

From the [GitHub Release](https://github.com/VUA-Project/VUA/releases/tag/v0.7.0) or the
[official download page](https://ph-r.com/vua/downloads/), choose a Windows x64 installer:

- **VUA-0.7.0-windows-x64-setup.exe**: the small online installer. It includes VUA and AMF and
  fetches the matching runtime from a reachable mirror during installation.
- **VUA-0.7.0-windows-x64-offline.exe**: the same version with the runtime included, for offline
  or unsteady networks.

To update from v0.6.7, finish your work, exit VUA and run the 0.7.0 installer. It brings the
host to 0.7.0 and the AMF module to **0.2.0**; AMF 0.2.0 needs host 0.7, so the in-app module
update on v0.6.7 does not offer it. Your profile, library, projects, Recipes and account data
stay in place. Later AMF updates within 0.7 arrive through Settings → Modules or About.

The installers are not Authenticode-signed yet; the release manifest's Ed25519 signature verifies
the update source. Unity, the VRChat SDK, Steam, VRChat and each tool come from their own
official sources.

## What's new

### Avatar production (AMF)

- **From the materials room to a finished Avatar.** Choose an Avatar and the outfits that fit it,
  and VUA opens a production line. Each line has its own Unity work project; your original
  packages stay untouched.
- **The workshop.** A line moves through stations: materials, inspection, plan, assembly and the
  quality check. VUA recommends the Avatar and outfit prefabs and says why. Outfits are bound with
  Modular Avatar, and each gets a menu switch. Previews show the result from the front, side and
  back.
- **Permission modes.** "Ask before changes" (the default), "Decide on its own" and "Full access"
  set how far a line runs without you. Full access warns first.
- **Shaders and packages.** lilToon, Poiyomi Toon (by its version line) and UnlitWF install from
  their official feeds when a material needs them. Packages you get yourself come with a link to
  their official page.
- **Material tuning.** Align the outfit's lilToon brightness limits with the Avatar's, so both
  brighten and darken together across worlds.
- **Changes and recovery.** Change a finished line's outfit or base Avatar, delete a line, stop
  and continue Unity work, and restore after an interrupted step. Every change keeps a restore
  point, and a failed change restores itself.
- **Work queue.** Unity work waits in a visible queue; up to three lines can run side by side
  (Settings in the workshop).
- **The SDK step in plain words.** VUA runs the VRChat SDK's own Avatar check, groups its items
  into "fix before uploading", reminders and notes, shows the performance rank, and runs the SDK's
  Auto Fix from the release page. After an upload, VUA reads the check again, so a fix you made in
  the SDK panel shows up.
- **The release room.** A 60-frame turntable album, and a managed upload through the SDK in the
  open Unity Editor after you confirm the content rights in VUA. Each upload is recorded with its
  VRChat Avatar ID or the SDK's reason.
- **Terms of use.** The library's product drawer reads a product's VN3 terms and shows what each
  use allows, with the creator's special note first. The release room checks a line's Avatar and
  outfits together for the use you choose.
- **Recipes.** Save a finished line as a Recipe (materials by content, choices, adjustments) and
  apply it to another line.
- **Unity and its license.** VUA starts Unity Hub before Unity work, recognizes the Microsoft
  Store (MSIX) Hub, and shows the license state with one next step.

### Experimental VRChat sign-in

Off by default, under Settings → Experimental. VUA explains the risk first: VRChat's terms ask
players to keep their own credentials, and third-party sign-in can lead to account action. After
you accept, you sign in inside VUA, with VRChat's two-step verification. A managed upload then
starts with the SDK already signed in, and the release room checks the uploaded Avatar against
your VRChat Avatar list. Sessions and remembered passwords are encrypted per Windows user.

### Tools and AI agents

- **Six Steam tools** share one connection: VRCFaceTracking, Space Calibrator, OVR Advanced
  Settings, OVR Overlay Translator, OVR Toolkit and OyasumiVR — discovery, install handoff, launch
  and status.
- **Local MCP connection.** External AI agents can read VUA's facts and request actions; you
  approve each request in VUA. Results are compact, a line's full facts are an MCP Resource, and
  errors say whether a retry is safe. `vua_status` names the exact build.

### Other improvements

- The play network tile measures each region on three connections and reports the fastest
  response, so repeated tests agree on the closest region.
- Task rows stay in place while downloads progress; production records live in Settings; VUA asks
  before quitting during a task.

## Verified, and what still waits

Real runs on the author's machine (2026-10-16 to 10-19):

- An Avatar with an outfit (Meiyun with the T-shirt & shorts) was prepared, assembled with correct
  lilToon shading, built by the SDK and uploaded privately twice through the managed upload.
- A material whose shader was missing received lilToon from its feed.
- With Unity Hub closed, VUA started it and Unity received its license.
- An external AI agent drove queueing, cancelling, stop and continue, a material change, deleting
  a line and an upload, with the author approving each request.

Awaiting the author's checks: the experimental sign-in with a real account; Poiyomi Toon; the
brightness alignment in a dark world; two lines side by side; changing a line's base Avatar;
VN3 terms on real products; the regional latency on several networks.

Built into the engine for upcoming workshop screens: a line's version history (restore any of
its last 50 states), saved outfit fits, and face tracking add-ons. Their screens come in a later
release.

This release is not a clean-machine, PICO USB/Wi-Fi, complex-material or four-language human
acceptance. Regional latency remains an HTTP reference to each region, not VRChat's room Ping.
